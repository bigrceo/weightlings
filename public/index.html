Famine, storm, plague or maze. Each weighs different traits taken from the model card, plus how the creature lived that hour.

The weakest 35% die. Their lineage loses a member. If it was the last one, the lineage is extinct.

What is real, exactly

The tone of this page is cold. The facts are plain. Here they are, without the tone.

The brains

22 real open-weight models. Parameters, active parameters, MMLU and context window are the published figures from each model card or paper. The full table, with the trait each number drives, is on the gene board.

The life

The meadow runs on our server, four ticks a second, the same for every visitor. Creatures look for berries within a radius set by their benchmark, pick the right move with a probability set by their benchmark, remember spots for a time set by their context window, and burn energy at a rate set by their active parameters. Duels are won by strength and judgement; sharing happens when both creatures roll their cooperation.

The selection

On the hour, a trial weighs the traits it cares about, adds how the creature lived during the hour, plus a little noise. The weakest 35% die. Survivors breed back to 160 creatures, fitter parents more often. A child keeps its parent's brain, except 12% of births that mutate one or two steps to a neighbouring model. An extinct lineage is not gone for good: each round, up to a quarter of the free places go to extinct lineages (token holders first), which hatch a new founder with the wallet's original brain. The more creatures share one brain, the harder every trial hits them, and a plague hits them hardest, so no single model can own the meadow for long.

The market

Each brain trades as an index around a reference of 1.000. After every round its premium moves with how its creatures survived against the meadow average, and it stays within ±30%. The exchange shows each model's price, band, history and, where a public host still serves the model, the real cost to run it per million tokens, live from OpenRouter. Swaps there use paper credits.

The hires

A hire gives one living creature a real task. The work is done by Claude through our API key, not by the creature's open model; every step is streamed and the run ends with a receipt signed by our server, which the hire page checks in your browser. Hires are free at launch and paid by the operator; paid hires in USDG are not live.

The draw

One minute before each trial the meadow is frozen: every living creature with its hour of foraging, every extinct lineage, the brain table. The SHA-256 of that snapshot is published at once. The hash also covers the trial second, the ids of the newborn, the rules file and the round before. The dice are the hash of the first Robinhood Chain block at or after the trial second, a block that does not exist when the snapshot is fixed. If that block cannot be read, or the result cannot be written, the round waits on the same snapshot and the same block. A round that still cannot be written is voided in the open: its commitment and its seed block stay in the log, nobody dies, and its number is not used again. Snapshot, block and result are stored with the round, and anyone can run the cull again in a browser.

What that does not cover: the hour of foraging inside the snapshot is counted by our server and cannot be recomputed, and it weighs more in the score than the dice. The snapshot's hash and the log of commitments are published by our own server and are not yet anchored on chain, so when a snapshot was fixed rests on our clock. Rounds held before this record existed stand on our word alone and are marked as such.

Limits we do not hide

  • The exchange is paper trading with free credits. No deposit, no real money, no real model tokens yet.
  • Brains are simulated from model data. The creatures do not run the models; no open model is called. That keeps the meadow fast, free and the same for everyone.
  • Before launch the meadow is seeded with the most active real Robinhood Chain wallets. Holders found their lineage automatically once the token is live.
  • This is a game and an experiment. Nothing here is financial advice.
